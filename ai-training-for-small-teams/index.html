AI training for a small team without a training day

Most teams of five to fifty have two people who use AI well and the rest who ask it the odd question. A training day costs a day and fades in a week. Here is the cheaper, stickier way.

The numbers behind the problem

Only 39% of people who use AI at work were trained by their employer (Microsoft Work Trend Index 2024, 31,000 workers in 31 countries). Where managers actively support AI use, 78% of staff use it frequently; where they do not, 44% (Gallup, April 2026). Among German firms, 43% offer no AI training at all and only 21% of employees have attended an in-company AI course (Bitkom, February 2026). The gap is not tools; it is a shared path and a manager who asks about it.

What the EU AI Act asks

Since 2 February 2025, Article 4 of the EU AI Act applies to every company whose staff use AI tools, including ChatGPT or Copilot. As amended in 2026 it asks employers to take measures to support the development of AI literacy; it sets no required level and no certificate. A shared learning path with a record of who did what is one simple measure you can document. Nothing on this page is legal advice, and no product makes a company "compliant"; it gives you something concrete to point to.

The method: one path, one weekly check-in

  1. Everyone gets the same guide. Eight levels, two a week, from "what AI actually is" to building a small tool without code. Same vocabulary across the team from week one.
  2. Fifteen minutes a week, on the calendar. Each person brings the practice task of the week: a real email rewritten with the prompt formula, a spreadsheet explained by AI, a research report produced in ten minutes. The task output is your record that the level was done.
  3. The two people who already use AI well lead the check-in. They answer questions; the manager does not have to be the expert.
  4. Level 3 decides the budget. Everyone compares the paid plans on their own tasks and the team pays for at most one tool per person, with a reason.
  5. Month two: pick one workflow and automate it. Levels 6 and 7 turn the most repetitive task in the team into something that runs on its own.
