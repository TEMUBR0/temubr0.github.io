ChatGPT for beginners: the five things to do in your first hour

Most first hours with ChatGPT end with a mediocre answer and a shrug. These five habits are what separate the people who get real work out of it from the people who tried it once.

Before you type anything: what it is

A chatbot predicts the most likely next words from patterns in its training. It does not look anything up unless it searches the web. That is why it can be fluent and wrong at the same time: check facts, figures, names and sources before you use them. And never paste passwords, other people's personal data or confidential company data into a tool your company has not approved. Those two sentences are Level 0 of the guide, and they are the reason the rest works.

1. One topic, one chat

Open a new chat for every topic and give it a name. A long chat that mixes your holiday, a client email and a spreadsheet formula gets worse with every message, because the model is dragging all of it along. Short, named chats are the single cheapest improvement available.

2. Upload instead of describing

Attach the PDF, the photo, the screenshot. "Here is the contract, what are the three risks for the tenant?" beats three paragraphs of your own summary every time. The free version lets you upload files; use it.

3. Use the formula

Task, context, example, persona, format, tone. "Write a reply (task) to this supplier who is two weeks late (context, attach the thread); here is an email of mine you should sound like (example); you are a calm operations manager (persona); five lines, no bullet points (format), firm but polite (tone)." One example of the result you want beats a paragraph of description.

4. Steer, do not restart

The first answer is a draft. "Shorter." "More formal." "You missed the deadline." "Redo point 2." For anything that matters, say "ask me any questions you need before answering" and let it interview you first. People who restart from scratch each time never get past mediocre.

5. Write your custom instructions

In the settings there is a box for who you are and how you want answers. Fill it once: your role, your company, your languages, the tone you want, the things it should always do (give dates, cite sources, stay under 200 words). Every new chat then starts informed. Dictate it if you can; spoken instructions are longer and richer, which is what you want here.

What comes after the first hour
