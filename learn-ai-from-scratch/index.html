How to learn AI from scratch in 2026: a one-month plan for non-technical people

If you have used ChatGPT for the odd question and stopped there, this is the order that works. Eight levels, two a week, about six and a half hours of video in total, no coding at any point.

Why most people stall

Not for lack of material. YouTube holds the best AI teachers in the world for free; the problem is the ten thousand other videos around them, most outdated, too technical, or selling something. People watch a 40-minute "complete guide", retain two tricks, and never practise on their own work. The fix is sequence plus practice: short videos in a set order, and one real task from your own job at the end of each step.

The one-month plan

  1. Week 1, Level 0: what AI actually is. Where it comes from, what a chatbot does when it answers (it predicts likely words from patterns, it does not look things up unless it searches), why it can sound certain and be wrong, and what you must never paste into one. 48 minutes. Practice: ask ChatGPT about something you know very well and catch one mistake.
  2. Week 1, Level 1: first steps with ChatGPT. One topic per chat, upload a file or a screenshot instead of describing it, talk to it with your voice, and dictation apps that type into any program. 33 minutes. Practice: upload a document from your own work (personal data removed) and ask for a five-line summary.
  3. Week 2, Level 2: talk to AI properly. The prompt formula (task, context, example, persona, format, tone), steering with follow-ups instead of restarting, making the AI ask you questions first, and custom instructions so it knows who you are in every chat. 33 minutes. Practice: write your custom instructions, then rewrite one real email with the formula.
  4. Week 2, Level 3: meet the other tools. Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and NotebookLM, what the free versions give you, what paying adds. 1 hour 27. Practice: run the same task in two tools and keep score.
  5. Week 3, Level 4: AI for real work. Spreadsheets, research reports in minutes, presentations from notes. 43 minutes. Practice: one spreadsheet and one report from your actual week.
  6. Week 3, Level 5: build your own assistants. Projects and Gems that know your documents and your rules. 34 minutes.
  7. Week 4, Level 6: agents, folders and automation. What an agent is, how it connects to your apps, things that run without you. 1 hour 19.
  8. Week 4, Level 7: build what does not exist yet. Claude Code for non-coders: a small tool for the thing that slows you down every week. 37 minutes.

What to pay for, and when

Nothing until Level 3. The free versions of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini carry the first levels. From Level 3 you compare the paid plans (about EUR 20 a month each, as of October 2026) on your own tasks and pay for at most one. Levels 6 and 7 need a paid Claude plan for Cowork and Claude Code.

Two habits that make the difference

Keep a word notebook. Every term you cannot explain (token, context window, agent, MCP) goes on a list; at the end of each level, ask the AI to explain each one in two sentences with an example. Check the date of every video. Facts about how AI works age well; videos about app screens and best practices age in months. When an old video and a recent one disagree, trust the recent one.
